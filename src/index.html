About me

I’m a retrained Product Designer with most of my experience in enterprise web design.

I started design inspired by stories of how designers shape a better world by blending creativity, empathy and hands-on problem-solving. A few yers in the field, I can see the rise of AI reshaping the role I once imagined. Fascinating times, absolutely worth the ride.

The road ahead

As a designer I want to stay focused on what truly matters: solving the right problems. I want to drive meaningful impact by crafting user-centered solutions, amplified by AI to accelerate iteration and validate ideas.

Enterprise UX

I've been primarily working in the B2B landscape.
Enterprise design is less about trends and more about clarity. It’s about solving complex problems efficiently, making steady progress and delivering real value in environments that are slow-moving and high-stakes.

Designing by vibe

The consumer world is more about the energy, where design is bold, emotional and fast-moving. I try to mix in some structure to create products that feel as good as they function.

Enterprise UX

Designing for enterprise is about delivering steady value and improving workflows without disrupting what already works. Mainly for web interfaces.

Challenges while designing for enterprise:


  • Understand specialized workflows to design for highly technical users.
  • Work within technical constraints and legacy systems.
  • Handle shifting priorities and trade-offs.
  • Embrace incremental delivery over chasing perfection.
  • Closely collaborate with engineers and product managers.

Customer pain points:


  • Diverse data points at a glance.
  • Simplified complexity, but keep advanced configuration capabilities handy.
  • Investigate issues in depth without leaving the interface.
  • Monitoring, debugging and analysis in one place.
  • Contextual guidance, but don’t make it feel like onboarding forever.
  • Improvements, but no disruptions of familiar workflows.
  • Alignment to other familiar tools and interaction patterns.

The design process I (usually) follow:


  1. Learn Gather information, leverage all possible sources without forming an opinion yet.
  2. Understand Listen and observe. Consider the context and make sure not to just build faster horses.
  3. Ideate Other people will know other things. Avoid to jump straight to the solution.
  4. Build A solid UX spec is key. Sometimes I’ll start from the north star and work down to an MVP.
  5. Improve My love goes to the human, not the design. I’ll test, validate, measure and improve.
